# Teaching pressure and research output: note for the instructor

Group A (S. Carone, F. Fragnito, A. Villani), project 4. 23 November 2026.

## 1. Question and design
Intervention: an externally imposed rise in enrolled students at fixed initial faculty, compared with no imposed increase. Outcome: log Scopus publications in year t, with exposure measured in t-1. Population: the 61 universities in the dataset, 2021-2023 (60 in the regressions, which need the 2018 field shares). We compare association benchmarks without and with cost control. Their intended targets are the total response at fixed faculty and the response at fixed faculty and spending; neither is causally identified here. Students, faculty and costs share the same annual lag, so their ordering relative to assignment is unknown. Spending could mediate a response through fees, or reflect pre-existing resources. The working DAG is in `output/figures/fig_16_causal_dag.pdf`.

## 2. Findings: association benchmark
- The exposure, log(students/faculty), varies almost only between universities: 93.8% of its sum of squares is between universities; net of university and year effects its SD is 0.037 against 0.229 overall (2.6% of the variation, before partialling out faculty and costs). In 2020-2022 the ratio rose in only 17 of 120 year-on-year changes; its frequent decline is consistent with growing faculty.
- OLS with university-clustered SEs: -0.37 with year effects only, +0.17 with 2018 field shares, +0.01 (SE 0.35; 95% CI -0.69 to 0.71) with university and year effects. Adding costs changes the estimates by less than their standard errors. The sign comes from comparisons between universities; the within-university estimate is uninformative.

## 3. Frontier evidence
- SFA (instructor's script 06, diagnostics in g4/11): the normal-half-normal fit with the load in the frontier is numerically unstable. Across 32 starting values and the explored likelihood profile, no reliable interior solution was found; 23 starts reach the operational boundary criterion. This is numerical evidence of a boundary problem, not a proof of global non-identification. In the scale equation (NHN_Z) the load is associated with more inefficiency (0.49 after refitting), but the university-clustered SE is 0.54. This scale coefficient is not an output elasticity and cannot be compared numerically with the OLS coefficient.
- Nonparametric frontier (FEAR; inputs faculty and deflated costs in t-1, output publications in t): fast tests with 1000 sample splits reject neither convexity nor constant returns, so the pre-specified rule selects DEA with CRS (a bootstrap convexity test with 10 splits and 200 replications gives p = 0.63 and 0.69). Median Farrell output score 1.31; 3 of 183 university-years are efficient; in the pooled frontier the median worsens from 1.25 (2021) to 1.40 (2023). Annual frontiers give rank correlations of 0.98-1.00 and medians of 1.18, 1.29 and 1.28: the 2023 worsening largely disappears when each year has its own frontier. This is consistent with a shift common to all universities in 2023 (for example, incomplete indexing of the most recent year), but annual frontiers are different reference technologies, so the comparison does not prove a common shift or an indexing problem. Extraction dates and source coverage need verification. Pooled tests and intervals treat repeated university observations as independent, an unresolved inference limitation; 200 bootstrap replications also trigger FEAR's documented warning.
- Robustness: nominal costs, faculty plus fellows, and dropping the ap outliers give medians of 1.31-1.33 and rank correlations of 0.91-1.00. Without the three humanities universities convexity is rejected, the rule selects FDH (41% efficient) and the rank correlation falls to 0.81.
- Measurement question: the three humanities universities (Napoli L'Orientale, Siena Stranieri, Perugia Stranieri) have very low observed publication counts. Scopus includes books and monographs ([Elsevier content policy](https://www.elsevier.com/products/scopus/content/content-policy-and-selection)), but its coverage of Italian-language humanities monographs is likely to be limited, which may contribute to the low counts (to be verified); the workbook metadata define publications as indexed documents attributed through AF-ID. We need to verify affiliation coverage, document filters and extraction dates before attributing the low counts to missing coverage. Exclusion is a sensitivity check, not a verified data repair.
- Efficiency is hump-shaped in the teaching load, but this is a comparison between universities, not an effect of the load.

## 4. Idea A versus idea B
- **A, teaching as an output** (publications and graduates). Pro: treats teaching as a legitimate output, as the project card suggests. Con: at the same sample and technology assumptions, adding an output can mechanically increase efficiency expressed as 1/Farrell (decrease the Farrell expansion factor); graduates are lagged and do not measure teaching quality. This does not isolate teaching pressure.
- **B, teaching load as an environmental factor Z**. Pending approval: a limited conditional-reference analysis, bandwidth sensitivity and a separability diagnostic, with estimator and assumptions checked against the final sample. Pro: explores whether the production reference depends on teaching load. Con: Z is not exogenous; reference groups and bandwidth matter; a separability test does not identify a causal mechanism. Cross-validation, an order-m grid and high/low-load mean tests are not part of the current minimal plan.

## 5. Proposal: idea B
B would be a descriptive extension concerning the production environment. It could inform the discussion of teaching obligations, but cannot establish that teaching causes lower research or that observed shortfalls are explained by legitimate obligations. The project card requires discussing joint teaching-research production; it does not require implementing both A and B. We await the instructor's choice before adding an extension.

## 6. Draft data request and causal design
- For the chosen student-demand intervention: admissions caps by programme and year, decision and announcement dates, allocation rules, enrolment and initial staff. A cap change is a candidate source of variation only after showing that its allocation is independent of research prospects and documenting fee, funding and student-composition responses.
- Extraordinary hiring plans would define an alternative staff/teaching-relief intervention, not the same intervention as more students at fixed faculty. Investigating this alternative would require plan-specific allocations, dates, eligibility and the full allocation rule. Removing research-related components of a formula does not by itself establish exogeneity or an exclusion restriction.
- Source verification of the 2023 ADJUNCT FACULTY values, which are zero for all 61 universities in the workbook (no zeros in 2020-2022); until verified, 2023 adjunct data are treated as missing and no adjunct extension is estimated.
- Teaching hours per faculty member (course catalogues) instead of students per faculty, and department-level output including books (institutional repositories). Spillovers through fees, staff funding and student composition must be documented before assuming exclusion.
